What a third and a fourth expert add to the action head

Adding a wrench expert and then a tactile expert to the policy's two-expert action head lowers the held-out joint error from 15.87 to 15.20 mrad, about 4%. The gain repeats on every training seed. The control runs show it does not come from the wrench history or from the tactile world model's forecast.

Update, 19 Nov 2026. This page describes the 8,000-step study. Trained for 50,000 steps, the four-expert model reaches 13.95 mrad and is the released model; the same model without force reaches 14.91 mrad and the two-expert model 14.34 mrad. The video world-model rollouts were repeated with the released model for three seeds. Current numbers: results page · checkpoints. The model

One encoder pass, 19 query slots, and a head that mixes experts

The policy reads three camera views and a short history of joint positions, fingertip forces and joint torques. It predicts the next 16 joint targets (0.53 s at 30 Hz) for both arms and both hands, 58 joints in all. Next to the action it forecasts the fingertip forces for the same 16 steps and makes three decisions about the near future.

  • Vision is frozen. Each 224 × 224 image goes through DINOv2 ViT-S/14 once, offline. Its patch features are pooled to a 4 × 4 grid plus the class token, so one camera is 17 tokens of width 384.
  • Actions are relative. The target is the commanded joint position minus the current joint position, divided by each joint's spread in the training data.
  • Force is predicted as a change. The force head outputs the difference from the force measured now, in units of 10 N. A model that outputs zero therefore predicts that the force stays as it is.
  • Decision slots feed the gate. The same three slots that are trained to say whether contact is coming also choose how much each expert counts. On held-out frames the two-expert model calls contact-in-0.33-s correctly 97.5% of the time and the phase 95.1%.
The action head

Four experts, two kinds

All experts propose the full 58-joint action for every chunk slot. They differ in what they can look at beyond the slot itself.

ExpertExtra inputOutputsOwn loss
Plain A, plain BNone. They read the chunk slot only.58 joint targetsNone. Trained only through the mixed action.
Wrench (third)One token for the current joint positions and one token per fingertip holding its last 10 frames of force and torque. The chunk slots attend to these 11 tokens. The design follows the FACET wrench encoder.58 joint targets and the next 60-D fingertip wrenchAction error of its own proposal, plus 0.1 × wrench error
Tactile (fourth)16 latents of 128 numbers from the frozen TacForeSight tactile world model: 8 encode the fingertip deformation images of the last 8 frames, 8 are its forecast for the next 8 frames (0.27 s).58 joint targetsAction error of its own proposal

The gate is a single linear layer on the three decision slots followed by a softmax. It gives one weight per expert per frame, shared by all 16 steps of the chunk. The final action is the weighted sum.

The tactile world model is the only place where the fingertip camera images enter the policy. Everything else uses the 6-number force and torque reading per fingertip. The world model was trained on the eraser task only, so its latents are less reliable on the other two tasks.

SharpaDex v1.0, 30 Hz. 10% of each task's episodes are held out, spread over recording sessions, and never trained on. A frame counts as in contact when any fingertip force exceeds 1 N. The contact share is of all frames of the task (last row: held-out frames).

Objective

  • Action: mean absolute error of the mixed action, weight 1.0.
  • Force forecast: mean absolute error of the force change, weight 0.5.
  • Decisions: phase, contact-soon and force-level classification, weight 0.2.
  • Wrench expert: its own action error plus 0.1 × wrench error, weight 1.0.
  • Tactile expert: its own action error, weight 1.0.
  • Optimiser: AdamW, learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁴ with 500 warm-up steps and a cosine decay, weight decay 0.01, batch 256, gradient clipping at 1.0, mixed precision.
  • Budget: 8,000 steps for every model on this page unless stated. That is about 4.4 passes over the 465,256 training frames and takes 11 to 12 minutes on one L40S GPU.
  • Seeds: every variant is trained three times (seeds 0, 1, 2). All three share the same held-out episodes.
  • Frozen parts: the image encoder and the tactile world model are not updated. Their outputs are computed once and cached. The tactile latents use only what the robot has at time t; the forecast is conditioned on the last command sent, held constant.
  • Same recipe for all three models: nothing but the expert switches changes between the two-, three- and four-expert runs.

Where the gain comes from

Not from the wrench history, and not from the tactile forecast

Each new expert has a control run that keeps the expert, its own loss and its size, and removes the special input.

  • Wrench history adds nothing measurable. The third expert helps just as much when it cannot see any force history. What remains is an extra expert that attends to the current joint positions and has its own action loss.
  • The tactile forecast adds nothing measurable. This fits an earlier check of the tactile world model: forces read from its forecast were less accurate than assuming the force stays as it is (1.00 N against 0.29 N).
  • The fourth expert's source is not isolated. Its control still sees the latents of the measured fingertip images. A run with no tactile tokens at all was not made, so the gain could come from the fingertip images or simply from one more expert with its own loss.

How the gate uses the experts

plain experts A + Bwrench experttactile expert

Mean gate weight on held-out frames, three seeds.

  • The new experts take over. With three experts the wrench expert carries 92% of the weight. With four, the wrench and tactile experts carry 93% between them, and the plain experts are almost unused outside contact.
  • The mixture beats its parts. In the four-expert model the wrench expert's own proposal scores 15.51 mrad and the tactile expert's 15.80. Their gated mix scores 15.20.
  • In the two-expert model the split is not stable. Which plain expert takes more weight in contact differs from seed to seed.

In the video world model

Closed-loop check in PrimeSim: done for the two-expert model only

Held-out error compares one prediction with one recorded action. It says nothing about what happens when the policy acts on the result of its own commands. To look at that without a robot, the policy is run inside PrimeSim, a video model that generates the three camera views from joint commands.

The three- and four-expert models have not been run this way. PrimeSim produces camera pictures only. It has no fingertip force, no wrench history and no fingertip images, which are exactly the inputs of the two new experts. The results below are for the two-expert model.

Update, 19 Nov 2026: the released four-expert model has since been run in the loop, with its force inputs replayed from the recording. All 36 videos, three seeds on three tasks: rollout videos.

The world model

  • Base: PrimeSim on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, generating head and both wrist views together.
  • Fine-tuning: a rank-32 LoRA plus the action-input layers, 6,000 steps on the eraser task and 6,000 more on all three tasks, on windows of 33 frames at 10 Hz.
  • Commands: PrimeSim expects hand poses. A small fitted network turns joint commands into hand poses (0.5 to 1.5 mm and 0.1 to 0.2° error), and a fitted response model predicts the next joint state from the commands (17.9 mrad over all joints, 3.2 for the arms).

The loop

  • Start: one real frame, 3.2 s into a held-out recording, and the two joint states before it.
  • Every 1.5 s: the policy sees the latest generated frames and is called three times, 0.5 s apart. The commands are given to the video model, and its last frame becomes the next observation.
  • Force inputs: the tactile and torque readings are replayed from the recording at the same time stamp. They match the imagined scene only while the rollout stays close to the recording.

  • No task success is measured. The videos look coherent, with robot, table and objects intact, but nothing checks whether the plate is wiped or the plug is in.
Limits

What these results do not show

  • Open-loop error is not task success. A 4% lower joint error on recorded frames has not been shown to change behaviour on a robot or in simulation.
  • The three- and four-expert models have no closed-loop result. Running them in PrimeSim needs simulated wrench history and fingertip images, which do not exist yet.
  • The expert count and the expert type change together. There is no run with three or four plain experts, so "more experts" and "an expert with its own loss and cross-attention" are not separated. A single plain expert was only tested on the eraser task alone, where it scored 19.25 mrad against 19.08 for two.
  • Controls at one training budget. The control runs and the three-expert model exist only at 8,000 steps. At 25,000 steps only the two- and four-expert models were trained.
  • Tactile world model trained on one task. Its latents are out of distribution on the plug and block tasks, and its forecast was not accurate on the task it was trained on.
  • Three seeds. Seed spread is about 0.1 mrad, small against the 0.3 to 0.7 mrad gains but of the same size as the control differences.
