Facet-0 and our force policy, side by side

Facet-0 (PINE Lab, NTU) is a foundation model for contact-rich manipulation built on a large vision-language-action model. Ours is a small policy trained from scratch on 4.3 hours of SharpaDex. Both read force, predict force next to the action, and generate actions in chunks.

The two models agree on what to do with force and differ in scale and in how they learn after imitation. Facet-0's lead is its language-capable backbone and its reinforcement-learning stages. Ours has richer force sensing, measured ablations, and a size that trains in minutes. The one Facet-0 piece we have tested so far, the wrench encoder, did not help on our data.

Where each one is ahead

Facet-0's advantages

  • It understands instructions. Our model ignores its task token: swapping it for another task's changes error by only 0.1 to 0.5 mrad. A pretrained language model is the direct fix.
  • It can get better than its demonstrations. Stages 2 and 3 use reward. Ours can only copy the operator, so its ceiling is the operator.
  • It starts from broad pretraining. New objects and scenes cost it far less data. Our error still falls 6.5% per doubling of data with no sign of flattening.
  • Cartesian actions transfer between robots. Joint targets are tied to this robot.
  • Longer plans. 50 steps against our 16, with a schedule that switches to fine corrections when contact starts.

Our advantages

  • Richer force sensing. One wrist sensor cannot tell which finger is touching. Ten fingertips can, and joint torque is our most useful force signal: removing it costs 0.73 mrad in contact.
  • Every use of force is measured. Each can be switched off in the config and was ablated with three seeds. Facet-0's public code has no such breakdown.
  • Small and fast. About 400 times fewer trained parameters. A full comparison with seeds reruns in under an hour, and the model could run on the robot's own computer.
  • Readable decisions. The contact-soon slot is right 97.5% of the time 0.33 s ahead and could gate a controller.
  • Dexterous hands. 44 finger joints are in the action. Facet-0 controls a gripper.

Where each one is behind

Facet-0's disadvantages

  • Heavy. Training needs a multi-GPU setup and the pi0.5 base weights; inference needs a server next to the robot.
  • Thin force sensing. A wrist wrench mixes every contact on the hand into six numbers. It would not scale to a tactile hand without redesigning the wrench token.
  • Stage 2 depends on a judge that is not released. The reward comes from a vision-language model's opinion of subgoal completion, so judge errors become policy errors.
  • Stage 3 needs a robot and a person. It learns from human interventions on hardware.

Our disadvantages

  • No language. A new task means a new token and new data.
  • Frozen, small vision. DINOv2-small features cannot adapt to tactile-relevant detail such as where a fingertip meets a rim.
  • Imitation only, tested offline only. Nothing yet shows the model completing a task. Error against the operator is a proxy.
  • Under-trained at 8,000 steps. Tripling the steps helped more (−2.2 mrad in free motion) than force did.
  • Three tasks, one robot. Nothing here has been shown to transfer.
